League of Legends esports, in a corner of your screen.

For the pro match you can't watch: the score, the clock, the gold lead, how long until the next drake or Baron, and what just happened, at a glance. SmalLoL is an unofficial desktop companion for League of Legends esports. Free and open source.

Two updates this round

0.1.6: draft records, and Terminalol, which makes the bar look like a terminal. The installed app tells you about the update; one click installs it.

Draft records

Every game's picks and bans are kept. The data window has a new Champions tab: each champion's presence, picks, bans and win rate at this Worlds. A team's page lists its games one by one, with what it picked, what it banned and whether it won; a player's page lists every champion he has played, with wins and KDA. With spoiler-free on, results and win rates stay hidden.

Terminalol

Someone asked whether it could look like Teams. We made it look like a terminal instead: tick it in the settings and the bar becomes a terminal window running a sync job. Like top, it lists the ten players' champions, levels, K/D/A, CS, gold and health, then the towers, the latest log lines and the next drake and Baron; during the draft, both teams' picks and bans. No cards, no game windows; each part can be switched off; English by default; double-click for the bar.

One bar, two lines. That's enough.

The top line is where the game stands; the bottom line is what just happened. The buttons only appear when the mouse comes over, so it stays out of your way.

Only the big moments pop up.

Baron, a Soul, an inhibitor, a game won: a card in the corner of the screen. Towers, drakes and kills get a thin strip. A run of towers or a wave of level 6s becomes one strip that updates, not a pile.

Where the data comes from

Why the LoL version is harder than the MLB one. Basesmall, the MLB version, uses MLB's complete public data: every pitch, every runner on every base; baseball also goes one pitch at a time. League of Legends' official live data has only numbers such as the score, gold, kills, drakes and Barons. Which tower fell, who banned what during the draft: none of it is in the official data. The fuller live data (where each player is on the map, for one) is licensed commercially to partners. And the game runs in real time, with pauses for technical problems and even remakes.

So we run computer vision on a cloud server. A server watches the official stream around the clock: one frame a second, read with OpenCV feature and template matching for the towers on the minimap, the champion cards of the draft and the game clock. The clock gives the stream's delay, so the readings line up with the official live data (score, gold, kills, objectives, items) and merge into one document, which the app reads every five seconds. The service is open source too: smallol-cv.

By default the app uses the public instance; you can run your own and point the app at it in Settings. Champion and item names come from Riot Games' Data Dragon.

What we did after launch

The Windows version came out on 22 October. The first Swiss day was the first real test; we fixed things while watching.

  • The official data changed its rules. On the first match day the official live data began refusing requests that were too recent, and nothing came in for a while. Fixed that night; if it changes again, the service adjusts by itself.
  • A pause right at the start. The game clock ran six minutes ahead for a while. It now follows the scoreboard clock on the broadcast, so pauses and restarts line up.
  • Towers flashing "?", bans vanishing, a pick missing. A tower once named on the minimap now stays named; the bans vanished because the server lacked a package; Ornn and Karma, whose draft cards were hard to read, got their own handling.
  • Remakes and chronobreaks. Not seen in a real game yet, but handled and tested on simulated data.
  • A steadier server. A stuck process restarts itself; system updates run when no match is on; the data service moved to our own domain.
  • The Mac version (experimental). Signed and notarized by Apple; help testing it is welcome.

Still improving

Every day of Worlds brings something new. Something read wrong, something missing, a feature you want: tell us in GitHub Issues, or under the posts on PTT and Threads. This round's draft records and Terminalol both came from feedback.

Questions

Not here? Ask on GitHub.

Is this made by Riot Games?

No. SmalLoL is an independent fan project. It isn't endorsed by Riot Games and doesn't reflect the views or opinions of Riot Games or anyone officially involved in producing or managing Riot Games properties. It has nothing to do with any team either.

When can I download it?

The first version is still being prepared, for Windows first. Leave your email and you'll get one message when it is out. If you can't wait, you can build it from source with the instructions on GitHub.

Does it cost anything? Are there ads?

It is free and open source (Apache License 2.0), with no ads and nothing to pay for.

Is it ahead of the stream?

No. The data comes from the official live data and the broadcast picture, and both arrive a little after the stream. It is for knowing how the game is going without watching, not for learning results early.

Will it spoil results?

During a game the bar shows the score; that is what it is for. If you'd rather not see the results of games that are already over, turn on Spoiler-free in the data window and the schedule hides scores and winners.

Isn't it obvious at work?

Switch to low-key mode and it becomes a small grey strip of numbers that does not look like sports. It turns low-key by itself while you present, play full screen or have Do not disturb on, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+H hides every window.

Does it run on a Mac?

It has only been tested on Windows, and the first version is Windows only. A Mac version depends on how many people want one: pick Mac when you sign up and you'll hear when there is news.

How does it update?

The installed version tells you in the app when there is a new version, and one click updates it. No need to come back here to download.

Does it collect my data?

The app doesn't. No account, no analytics. It only reads match data from the data service and checks for new versions. Settings and the event history stay on your computer.

No account. No analytics.

The app talks to two places: the match data service (the public one by default, or your own) and the check for new versions. Only when you click "▶ Watch" does it open the stream address you saved, in your browser. Settings and the event history stay on your computer.
